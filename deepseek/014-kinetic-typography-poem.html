I
The tide turns over in its sleep,
and the harbour holds its glass.
II
The boats breathe against their ropes —
a slow phrase of wood, repeated.
III
The water learns the moon by heart,
and keeps the lesson to itself.
IV
What I mistook for silence
was a choir at half volume
below the deck of sound.
V
The dark is not a colour —
it is the sea, poured upward.
VI
Clouds unstitch their hems of moonlight
over the quiet station.
VII
The gulls have folded their white
notes into the cliff.
VIII
I keep saying the salt off my tongue,
finding only the almost your name.
IX
Stay until the last lamp drowns,
dripping light.
