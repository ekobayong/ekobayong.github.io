The greedy weaver: from the current nail, it tries every other nail, scores each candidate line by the ink it would remove, and pulls the best one — 0.13 of a line's worth of darkening, drawn in a translucent hairline with a shadow beneath.

Straight lines read as shade the way rain reads as a sea: enough of them, close enough together, and the eye stops seeing the string and starts seeing the face. That is the whole trick, and the whole craft.

To build it physically: a 60 cm birch board, 120 brass nails, and one very long reel of thread. The machine is patient; the maker needs to be too.
