Airship Meridian Spans Ocean in Sixty-One Hours

The great lighter-than-air fleet carrier Meridian, nigh on seven hundred feet from stem to stem, makes landfall over harbour light at a quarter past seven.
By Our Aeronautical Correspondent, H. J. Vane

At a quarter past seven o'clock this morning the dirigible Meridian, pride of the transatlantic lighter-than-air fleet, crossed the harbour bar with the ebbing tide beneath her and the whole sky above her. Sixty-one hours out of Lisbon, with two hundred and nine souls aboard and breakfasts still warm, she lowered her mooring lines into the waiting hands of half a thousand watching citizens, who had lined the quay since before dawn.

Her commander, Captain Elias Thorn, pronounced the crossing "a calm errand, and a short one," and immediately retired to his cabin, having been awake, his officers said, for the entire passage. The airship will lie at her mast until Monday, when she takes on mail, machinery and a consignment of oranges for the New York run.

The Meridian is the largest vessel ever to overfly the ocean in either direction, her envelope displacing more than twelve acres of gas, and her passenger deck fitted with a smoking room, a saloon with rosewood panelling, and — the papers are pleased to report — an electric lift.

GRAND HOTEL BLAZE — MANY SAVED BY FIRE LADDERS

Fire broke out in the Grand Hotel kitchens shortly after midnight; the alarm was tardy, the heroism was not. Guests on the upper floors were brought down by ladder and blanket, and a chambermaid is to be commended for carrying the register of arrivals into the street before any breakfast was lost.

RADIO SYMPHONY HEARD ACROSS THE PROVINCE

The Meridian Wireless Symphony, broadcasting from the university tower, was received last evening at distances of a hundred and forty miles, "with the violins unmistakable", by a wireless enthusiast in the mountains.

THE COUNCIL VOTES THE SPEED LIMIT, TWICE

After an evening of oratory — one alderman comparing the motor-car to the plague, another to the piano — the city council approved a twenty-mile speed limit by a standing vote, then immediately rescinded it by a voice vote, then restored it by acclamation, and adjourned to dinner.

RACING YACHTS, AND A ROW OF UMBRELLAS

The autumn regatta went ahead in a rising wind that the committee judged "part of the sport". The cutter Larkspur took the champion cup by half a length, to the great sorrow of the club's official scorer, whose umbrella had blown away at the starting gun.

SOCIETY: THE MASKED CONCERT AT HALCYON

Society was out in force last night at the Halcyon Observatory, where the winter series opened with masked quartets and a supper of cold ham. Mrs. Aldworth-Key wore her celebrated pearls, worn this season at every single occasion, and the dome was kept open to the sky between movements.

HARBOUR BOARD RECORD — AND THE AMUSEMENTS

The Harbour Board logged a record tonnage for the month ending yesterday, dirts and all. This evening: the Bijou presents The Silent Witness with orchestral accompaniment; Armstrong's Elephant Troupe remains at the Pavilion; and the Civic Orchestra offers Mendelssohn, whom nobody in the city has tired of.
