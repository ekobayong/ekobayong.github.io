HONEST DESIGN OR OR NOTHING

We reject the polished lie. Interfaces that hide their mechanics, that trade clarity for theatre, that apologise for being software — they are not design, they are decoration with better margins. This document is signed in ink, not gradients.

✳
* Terms apply to everyone, including us
I

Truth is the load-bearing wall

A button that looks pressed must be pressed. A number that looks live must be live. Every visual promise is a structural promise — the first lie cracks the whole building.

§01 · Substance
II

Show the seams

Loading is honest. A cursor is honest. The slow network, the failed save, the empty state — these are not failures of design. They are the seams where the product shows it is built by people.

§02 · Process
III

Weight is information

Heavy means important. Light means secondary. Grey means gone. Typography that refuses to choose a hierarchy is a paragraph with stage fright.

§03 · Hierarchy
IV

Never hide the undo

The destructive action, the irreversible workflow, the accidental delete — these demand a visible escape hatch, not a polite tooltip discovered after the damage.

§04 · Recovery
V

Speed is a feature

An interface that respects time respects its user. Every millisecond of theatre is stolen from someone's afternoon. Animation must earn its frames.

§05 · Tempo
VI

Default to plain

Before you add the flourish, ask what it does. Most of the time the answer is nothing. Plain is not a lack of design — it is design that has finished arguing.

§06 · Restraint
VII

The error belongs to the product

"User error" is a confession. When a human stumbles, the system stumbled first. The honest interface says: I wrote this message, I will fix this path.

§07 · Accountability
VIII

Delete the shadow

Drop shadows suggest depth where there is none, elevation where there is no hierarchy. The web is still a sheet of paper that scrolls. Be proud of the flat.

§08 · Form
IX

Design is a promise you keep

Every interface is a contract signed between the maker and the moment. Break the contract and no animation, no palette, no craft can buy back the trust. Keep the promise, and the plainest pixels become a place people choose to return to — not because it flatters them, but because it never lies to them.
