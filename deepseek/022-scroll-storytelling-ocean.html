Zone 01 · 0–200 m

Epipelagic — the sunlit zone

Down here light still rules. Photons reach perhaps two hundred metres, and almost everything that lives in the ocean lives on their charity: phytoplankton turn sun into sugar, krill eat the sugar, and the whole blue pantry of the pelagic zone is stocked from a single skylight.

The surface is not a boundary you can see from below. It is a ceiling of liquid glass, shimmering with pressure, through which the entire sky — and occasionally a whale — slides.

Zone 02 · 200–1 000 m

Mesopelagic — the twilight zone

Blue still arrives here, but it arrives tired. By five hundred metres a human eye would see only a dim, directionless dusk. The animals that live in this zone have answered the shortage of light with a surplus of invention: lanterns under their chins, lures on their foreheads, organs that switch on and off like rooms in a hotel.

Every night the largest migration on Earth passes through this water — billions of lanternfish rising to feed under the cover of darkness, then sinking back before the sun can find them.

Zone 03 · 1 000–4 000 m

Bathypelagic — the midnight zone

No sunlight has ever reached this floor. The only light is manufactured, and the only food is the slow snowfall of marine detritus — the remains of the sunlit world, falling past like a blizzard of ash. A sperm whale descending to hunt may pass a hundred metres of absolute night for every living thing it sees.

Pressure here would crush a submarine like a fist around a paper cup. The animals that call this dark home don't fight it; they simply don't have any air to compress.

Zone 04 · 4 000–6 000 m

Abyssopelagic — the abyssal zone

The abyss is the largest habitat on the planet and the least visited. Its floor is a softly rolling plain of sediment, interrupted by hydrothermal vents that cook the water to over 300 °C and feed, entirely without sunlight, gardens of tube worms and blind white crabs.

The Atolla jellyfish flashes crimson here as a burglar alarm — a cry for help aimed at any predator large enough to eat whatever is attacking it. Even in the dark, evolution has invented the distress call.

Zone 05 · 6 000–10 935 m

Hadalpelagic — the trenches

Named for Hades, because it seemed reasonable. The trenches are the deepest cuts in the planet's crust: the Mariana Trench alone could swallow Everest with more than two kilometres to spare. Life persists even here — pale snailfish the length of a hand, cruising the pressure of a thousand atmospheres as if it were weather.

A descent into the hadal zone is a descent into the oldest, quietest room on Earth — one that has never once seen the sun.

Light: noneTemp: ~1.1 °CPressure: 600–1 100 atm
