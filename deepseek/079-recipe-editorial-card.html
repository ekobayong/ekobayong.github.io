Saffron & Blood Orange & Tart

A butter crust the colour of late sun, a custard breathed on by saffron, and blood oranges cut to show their burgundy hearts. Best eaten the way winter insists: slowly, in company.

Method

  1. Cut the butter into the flour and sugar until it resembles breadcrumbs; add the egg and bring together into a disc. Chill for thirty minutes.
  2. Roll the pastry and line a 24 cm tart ring. Dock with a fork and blind-bake at 170°C for 18:00 until pale gold.
  3. Warm the cream and milk gently; add the saffron and its steeping water, and leave to infuse for ten minutes.
  4. Whisk the eggs and yolks together, then pour over the warm (not hot) cream through a fine sieve.
  5. Pour the custard into the blind-baked shell and bake at 150°C for 22:00 — the centre should tremble like a held answer.
  6. Supremes: cut the blood oranges into segments over a bowl, catching every drop of juice in the sugar.
  7. Cool the tart completely; 120:00 of patience is not optional.
  8. Arrange the segments in a slow spiral from the centre out — seven, then six, then the small ones — and brush with the syrup. Serve at room temperature, to people you like.
