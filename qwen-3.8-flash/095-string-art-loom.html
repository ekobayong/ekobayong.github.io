The greedy loom

01 · ALGORITHM

A greedy algorithm has no long-term memory and no regrets. Standing at one nail, the weaver tests every other nail on the frame, stretches an imaginary line across the fading photograph underneath, and asks a single question: how much darkness would this line still add? It takes the best answer, subtracts a little ink credit from the map, and moves on. Nothing is ever undone.

That is the whole trick. One straight line tells you almost nothing; three thousand greedy straight lines, each answering for the shadow the last one left behind, reconstruct a face. The minimum angular gap is the only foresight allowed — it stops the thread from stuttering back and forth over the same millimetre of wood.

Why straight lines read as shade

02 · PERCEPTION

Your eye is a terrible measuring instrument and a wonderful statistician. Where threads cross, density rises; where they run parallel, they blur into a grey wash. The visual cortex integrates thread counts per square millimetre and reports tone — the same bargain every engraver, newspaper etcher and pointillist struck with the viewer.

String art simply inverts the sign: instead of depositing ink in dark places, a dark thread on a light board is the ink, and the blank wood is the paper. Leave a region untouched and it reads as highlight. The portrait is not drawn; it is negotiated between what the thread covers and what the board keeps.

Building it for real

03 · SHOP NOTES

Print the target at 60 cm and tape it to a board of birch ply. Drive 180 flat-headed nails around the outline on a jig, 8–10 mm deep, so the heads sit just above the paper. Choose strong thread — 30-weight cotton perlé or fine embroidery floss — and wax it lightly so it slides without snapping.

Anchor with a double knot on one nail, then follow the export: hook, pull taut, wrap twice around the next nail, and keep the loops on the back tight and even. Rest the pattern every hundred threads; by three thousand the ghost of the photograph will surface on its own. Sand a coat of matte medium over the face when you are done, and the tension holds.
