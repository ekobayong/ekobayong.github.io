Saffron &
Blood Orange Tart

A saffron custard the colour of late light, laid into burnt-butter pastry and roofed with winter oranges — the tart that turns a grey January afternoon into something worth setting a table for.

  • 40 minPrep
  • 45 minBake
  • 2 hrs 40Rest
  • DevotedDifficulty

Ingredients

Makes
8 servings

Burnt-butter pastry

Saffron custard

Blood orange top

Method

  1. Bring the pastry together

    Rub the butter into the flour, sugar and salt until the mix looks like wet sand and smells faintly biscuity. Beat in the yolk and blood-orange zest with just enough iced water — a spoonful at a time — to make a smooth dough. Flatten, cling-wrap, and chill.

    45:00

    A cold dough rolls cleanly; a warm one shrinks from the tin.

  2. Line and blind-bake the shell

    Roll the dough 3 mm thick and ease it into a 23 cm fluted tin without stretching. Chill 15 minutes, then line with paper and beans and bake at 180°C fan until the edges are set and pale gold.

    18:00

  3. Dry the base

    Lift out the paper and beans, prick any bubbles with a skewer, and return the shell to the oven for a few minutes until the base no longer looks raw. Turn the tin once if your oven runs hot on one side. Cool on a rack, tin still on.

  4. Bloom the saffron

    Warm the milk until it just steams — never boils — and drop in the saffron. Leave it a slow ten minutes until the milk is the colour of marigolds and the kitchen smells of hay and honey.

    10:00

  5. Make the custard

    Whisk the yolks, sugar and crème fraîche to a pale ribbon. Strain in the saffron milk, then cook gently in a clean pan, stirring a figure of eight, until it coats the back of a spoon. Off the heat, squeeze out the gelatine and stir it through. Cool ten minutes, then pour into the shell and fridge.

  6. Roof it with oranges

    Cut the segments free of their membranes over a bowl, keeping the juices. Lay half over the just-set custard, chill until firm, then arrange the rest in a spiral from the outside in — shingled like a roof.

    40:00

    Reserve the bowl juices — they go into the glaze.

  7. Brew the glaze

    Simmer the water, honey, cinnamon and vinegar for a few minutes until slightly syrupy. Discard the cinnamon, stir in the reserved orange juices, and brush a thin lacquer over the fruit. It should set in minutes to a jewel shine.

    03:00

  8. Unmould and finish

    Leave the tart ten minutes out of the fridge, then release the tin rim with a warm knife. Curl a few bare saffron threads over the centre, dust the rim with a little icing sugar if you like, and serve on warmed plates.

Kitchen note

On saffron Bloom it in milk that steams but never boils — scorched threads turn bitter and mute the gold. A good pinch in a tart tin is enough: the spice should whisper, not shout.
