Honest design or nothing

Every shadow is a promise. Every rounded corner is an apology. This is a working document for interfaces that admit what they are: nine clauses, signed by the pixels themselves.

Read the clauses Sign it
*
* Terms apply to everyone, including us
01

Honest design is not a style

It is not a trend you can install and uninstall with a stylesheet. Honesty is a constraint you carry into every meeting: the interface must never claim more than it does, never hide what it costs, never dress a decision up as a feeling. When the box is a button, it looks like a button. When it is not, it does not.

02

Show the seams

A thing made by hand shows its joinery. A page built of parts should not pretend to be a single poured slab. Let the loading state be a loading state, let the error be an error, let the network be the network. Users forgive friction they understand and resent magic they don't.

material honesty
03

Weight is information

Type, colour and space are not decoration; they are how the page argues. Give the important sentence the heavy type and the loud colour, and give the footnote the grey it deserves. If two things matter equally, you have not decided what matters.

hierarchy
04

Delight must be earned

Animation is the interface bragging. Use it to explain cause and effect, never to flatter the designer. A bounce that teaches nothing is noise. The best motion is the motion you stop noticing the moment it has said what it came to say.

motion
05

Defaults are decisions

What ships on by default is what you believe in. Every opt-out is an argument you were too shy to finish. Choose the honest default, then let people leave loudly and easily — that leaving is also a feature.

consent
06

Speed is a civil right

The slow page steals from the poor and the impatient alike. Every megabyte is a tax on someone else's attention and bandwidth. Optimise like the reader is on a bus in a tunnel at 7am — because they are.

performance
07

The empty state is a promise kept

Nothing is the hardest thing to design well. A good blank page explains what belongs there, how to begin, and why the silence is not a bug. The worst interfaces are the ones that panic when there is nothing to show.

nothing
08

Make the undo honest

Destructive acts deserve their weight: a confirm that means it, an undo that really undoes, a label that names the consequence and not the euphemism. Never hide destruction behind a friendly verb.

recovery
09

If in doubt, say the truth plainly

When the brief is vague, when the client is nervous, when the deadline bites: resist the gradient of the lie. A plain sentence beats a beautiful one that is wrong. This clause outranks all others, including this one.
