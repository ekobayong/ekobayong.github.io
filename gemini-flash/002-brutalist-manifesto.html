HONEST
DESIGN
OR NOTH-ING

We reject the butter-smooth simulation of human empathy executed via CSS cubic-beziers. Interfaces are machines engineered by people to be operated by people. Draw the borders in ink, state every consequence, and cease apologizing for structure.

Read the 9 Clauses Sign Document
*
* Terms apply to everyone, including us
01

Weight is information

Thin hairline dividers indicate fear of commitment. If an element delimits two distinct operations, it must carry a visible, unyielding weight. Every stroke proves the existence of a boundary. Do not whisper layout choices when you can stamp them into the canvas.

02

Expose The Seams

An application is a sequence of joints, transitions, and state boundaries. Hiding these joints behind pillowy padding and synthetic gradients constitutes aesthetic fraud. Reveal how the software is assembled. Show where the grid fractures.

03

Never Ease In

Physical buttons click; relays fire instantly. Micro-animations that mimic wet clay waste human cognitive bandwidth. If a control changes state, it should slam into place immediately or step mechanically.

04

Borrow No Type

Five megabytes of webfonts pulled from remote tracking servers is an act of hostility against the user's network. System fonts were forged over decades by master typographers for raw screen legibility. Use them unapologetically.

05

Contrast is Honesty

Low-contrast light-gray text on white backgrounds is an exclusionary corporate gesture disguised as subtlety. High contrast affirms readability as an absolute right, not an optional accessibility setting toggled in menus.

06

Zero Blur

Soft shadows simulate imaginary lamp light. We do not work under sunlit porticos; we work on glowing phosphor grids. Shadows must be hard, offset planes of solid black ink.

07

Declare Every Cost

A button that initiates irreversible data destruction must not resemble a friendly secondary action. Danger is red; consequence is loud; permanence cannot be swept under an undo snackbar that evaporates in four seconds. Label every lever with its exact yield.

08

Keep The Ink

Four colors are sufficient for any complex machine: Paper for field, Ink for line, Acid for attention, Red for finality. Anything more is decorative indulgence.

09

Refuse The Consensus

Every software product is converging into an identical gray-rounded rectangle designed to offend zero venture capitalists. Break the grid. Refuse the template. An interface should look like it was drafted with a heavy steel ruler and an indelible felt marker by someone with a point to prove.
