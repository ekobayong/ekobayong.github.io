ZONE 01 // 0m – 200m // EPIPELAGIC

The Sunlight Zone

The sunlit surface waters represent less than five percent of the world ocean’s volume, yet they cradle the overwhelming majority of known marine life. Here, photons penetrate the clear upper layer, fueling billions of tons of microscopic phytoplankton that generate more than half of the oxygen breathed across our planet.

Vibrant coral gardens erupt in kaleidoscopic complexity, while vast schools of sardines, mackerel, and pelagic predators trace shifting thermal currents alongside green sea turtles and graceful manta rays. As daylight filters downward, warm turquoise steadily deepens into sapphire, until the final wavelengths of usable light fade into twilight.

ZONE 02 // 200m – 1,000m // MESOPELAGIC

The Twilight Zone

Past two hundred metres, sunlight diminishes into an eerie, monochromatic indigo twilight. This light is insufficient to sustain photosynthesis, but bright enough for predators with enormous tubular eyes to detect prey silhouetted against the surface. Every evening, this zone orchestrates the Diel Vertical Migration—the greatest synchronized movement of animal biomass on Earth—as billions of organisms rise to feed under the cover of night.

To avoid detection in this dim realm, creatures rely on counter-illumination. Lanternfish and deep-sea hatchetfish ignite rows of ventral photophores, precisely matching the color and intensity of downwelling light to erase their own shadows from hunters lurking in the cold depths below.

ZONE 03 // 1,000m – 4,000m // BATHYPELAGIC

The Midnight Zone

At one thousand metres, the last vestige of surface sunlight is permanently extinguished. The bathypelagic is an endless expanse of pitch darkness, near-freezing water, and immense hydrostatic pressure. The only illumination is born within the bodies of the organisms themselves: flashing luciferin bioluminescence, glowing fishing lures, and sparkling chemical smokescreens discharged to disorient foes.

Nutrients are exceedingly scarce, descending only as continuous flurries of "marine snow"—decaying organic matter and planktonic debris from above. Massive sperm whales dive thousands of metres into this midnight void on single breath-holds to clash with colossal squid, while anglerfish suspend luminous bacterial lures before gaping jaws armed with needle-sharp fangs.

ZONE 04 // 4,000m – 6,000m // ABYSSOPELAGIC

The Abyssal Zone

Spanning over sixty percent of Earth’s solid surface, the abyssopelagic drapes across vast plains of fine pelagic ooze and volcanic basalt. At pressures exceeding four hundred atmospheres, the physical properties of water shift; unadapted cell membranes would freeze into rigid solids and calcium carbonate shells would instantly dissolve.

Life endures through specialized piezolyte molecules like TMAO, which prevent enzymes from being crushed by water pressure. Here drifts the crimson Atolla jellyfish: its scarlet pigment absorbs all blue bioluminescence, rendering it completely invisible until disturbed, whereupon it unleashes a dazzling circular light show—a burglar alarm designed to summon larger predators to consume its attacker.

ZONE 05 // 6,000m – 10,935m // HADAL

The Hadal Trenches

Named after Hades, ruler of the underworld, the hadal zone consists of sheer V-shaped tectonic canyons where continental plates collide and plunge into the mantle. Far from barren wastelands, these subduction trenches act as organic funnel traps, collecting nutrient-rich sediments that tumble down the precipitous cliffs to nourish dense colonies of amphipods and xenophyophores.

At depths exceeding eight thousand metres lives the Mariana snailfish, the deepest-dwelling vertebrate known to science. Free of scales, rigid bones, and swim bladders, its soft, ghostly translucent flesh is packed with molecular stabilizers that thrive under the immense weight of an eight-mile column of ocean.

TERMINUS // 10,935m // CHALLENGER DEEP

Challenger Deep

You have reached the floor of the Mariana Trench, the deepest recorded scar on the Earth's lithosphere. Overhead rests ten thousand nine hundred and thirty-five metres of seawater, generating a crushing hydrostatic load of nearly 1,100 atmospheres—equivalent to the weight of an eight-ton locomotive concentrated upon every square foot.

Even here, in absolute detachment from the sunlit heavens above, chemosynthetic microbial mats thrive upon mineral seeps, sustaining ancient lineages that have survived multiple planetary extinctions in unbroken peace.

It is cold, and it is dark, and it is quiet, and it is alive.
