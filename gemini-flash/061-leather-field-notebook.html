19th Sept. — Stiffkey Freshes

A morning of sheer glass and retreating tides. Walked two miles past the sea kale mounds to where the channel empties itself into Blakeney harbour.

Found three whole whelk shells, intact and chalky-smooth. Skipped flat flint stones into the gut channel; seven skips was my record before the brine claimed it.

* Low water precisely at 07:18 GMT.
* Wind NW force 2, clearing to brilliant blue by midday.

17th Sept. — The Spit

A solitary metal-detectorist combed the shingle ridge like a heron wading in iron.

Out on the outer sandbank, eleven common seals rested like dark curved commas against the pale sand.

They watched us without moving, slick as wet basalt.

Saltmarsh Gutters

Cut through the creek-marsh before the flood tide filled the gutters. Redshank piping in panic at every three strides; sudden flurry of white wings.

Pressed sprigs of sea-lavender directly between the tide tables. Their petals look papery already, dried by sea-brine and wind before I even picked them.

Note: The mud here holds fast like warm pitch.
Keep strictly to the samphire beds.
14th Sept. — Cley Next-the-Sea

Set out at first dawn under a vault of damp pearl mist. The harbour was still, water level flush against the grey timber moorings.

Three little egrets stepped along the ditch like silhouettes cut from parchment. Nearby, oystercatchers kept their frantic sentinel chatter.

The tide has left ribbed ripple-bars across the sandbar, like raked zen sand on a giant scale.

The Old Collier Wreck

Half a mile west lies the timber frame of the wrecked schooner, blackened oak ribs protruding through the shingle like the skeleton of a great pilot whale.

Barnacles have cemented the rusted bolts. Sea anemones tucked into the shadow beneath the keel planking, waiting for high water.

Curlew call heard inland: long ascending whistle, melancholy and clear above the surf. Wind hauling East.
