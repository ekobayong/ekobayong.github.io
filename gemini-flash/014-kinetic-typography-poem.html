Scroll
I
The quiet tide forgets the sun, and drinks the evening cold.
II
A rib of silver cuts the floor where sunken bells unfold.
III
The black horizon pulls apart. No harbor lights remain.
IV
The ocean tilts its heavy chest; the drowned cathedrals sigh; a thousand miles of salt confess.
V
A well of ink without a floor fills up from underneath.
VI
Phantoms rise through miles of glass to break without a sound.
VII
The drift gathers what the wind tore loose across the shelves.
VIII
Every drowning syllable says almost your name.
IX
And all the dark returns at last to silence, dripping light.
