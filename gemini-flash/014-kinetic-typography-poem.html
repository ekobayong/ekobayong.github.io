I / IX
Scroll
I

The shore lets go
of the sun’s dry ache;
the black water
drinks the sky.

II

Low swells turn
beneath the shelf,
a heavy breath
without an edge.

III

Miles of salt, unbroken dark, a quiet older than our grief. still we listen.

IV

Down through shelves of frozen glass, the drowned bell swings unseen.

V

Cold water climbs
the quiet stones;
it fills the hollows
of the ribs.

VI

Pale sparks wake
beneath the keel,
a thousand fires
made of mist.

VII

What was broken
floats in pieces,
gathered back
by the long pull.

VIII

Every crest
repeats in foam
what the dark forgot—
almost your name.

IX

Now the quiet holds the tide, an ancient hand dripping light.
