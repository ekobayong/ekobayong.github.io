AIRSHIP MERIDIAN SPANS OCEAN IN SIXTY-ONE HOURS

Colossal Silver Leviathan Lands at Municipal Mooring Mast Amid Thunderous Cheering of 200,000 Spectators — Log Shows Flawless Navigation Through Atlantic Gyles.
— BY ARTHUR V. STERLING, SPECIAL CORRESPONDENT —

TOUCHING HER NOSE with majestic precision to the gilded cupola of the Municipal Mooring Tower at precisely forty-eight minutes past six o'clock this morning, the rigid airship Meridian achieved what aeronautical engineers throughout Christendom have hailed as the supreme conquest of the western ether. The mammoth craft, five hundred and eighty feet of ribbed duralumin and taut silvered linen, traversed the three thousand two hundred leagues between Cardington Downs and Meridian Bay in sixty-one hours and fourteen minutes, lopping nearly half a day from the record posted in August by the German Nordstern.

From dawn's earliest grey, a crowd estimated by Chief Constable Vane at not less than two hundred thousand souls choked every wharf, pier, roof-top, and church belfry commanding a vista of the roadstead. When the five Maybach diesel power plants throttled back to half-advance, and the great vessel tilted her empennage gracefully over the suspension towers, the blare of factory sirens and steam whistles from sixty tugs set up a din that rolled ten leagues inland.

"We encountered neither structural fatigue nor mechanical perturbation," stated Commander Sir Hugh Montagu-Cross upon disembarking into the elevator cage sixty feet above the quay. "Off the Grand Banks, a squall of sleet challenged our trim for three bells, yet the gyro-stabilisers held the carriage as level as a billiard cushion. My eighteen passengers sat down to devilled kidneys and vintage Burgundy while suspended three thousand feet above the foaming billows."

The passenger manifest included Viscount Hallowell, the Governor-General of the Bahamas, two directors of the Midland Railway, and Madame Vivienne Delacroix of the Paris Opéra Comique. The voyage inaugurates a bi-weekly post and passenger service which, according to the Ministry of Transport, will make the crossing of the North Atlantic a matter of weekend luxury before the turn of the decade.

GENEVA POWERS SIGN ARMS PACT ACCORD

GENEVA, Oct. 13 (By Cable). — In a midnight plenary convocation at the Palais des Nations, plenipotentiaries representing fourteen signatory realms affixed their seals to the Maritime Guarantee Accord. The protocols establish rigorous ceilings on auxiliary cruisers and submarine displacement. Premier Briand declared to the press that the settlement removes the last grave obstacle to continental pacification.

GOLDEN VAULT UNSEALED NEAR THEBES

CAIRO, Oct. 13 (Wireless). — Dispatches received at Alexandria confirm that Lord Abermarle’s expedition in the Valley of the Kings has breached the sealed antechamber of the Eleventh Dynasty Regent Menkheperre. Beneath a fallen limestone lintel, excavators uncovered nineteen alabaster urns intact, three bronze chariots inlaid with turquoise, and an intact electrum pectoral bearing the falcon insignia.

CALL MONEY DROPS TO 4½ PER CENT

NEW YORK, Oct. 14. — Heavy balances released by mid-month dividend disbursements precipitated an abrupt easing in call loan facilities on the financial district exchange today. Quotations opened at 5 per cent and receded steadily to 4½ per cent at the third call.

SPECTACULAR FIRE DESTROYS WING OF GRAND HOTEL

Heroic Rescues by Hook & Ladder Co. No. 4 Avert Tragedy as Ball-Room Cornice Falls into Courtyard.
— BY JULIAN C. CRANE —

FLAMES OF UNKNOWN origin, bursting forth shortly before two bells past midnight from the linen depository on the fourth storey of the Grand Hotel on St. Jude Boulevard, gutted the entire north mansard and sent nearly two hundred guests fleeing into the autumn rain in night attire.

The arrival of four steam pumpers under Assistant Chief MacIntyre prevented what threatened to become the most calamitous conflagration since the warehouse district blaze of 1918. Fireman Denis O'Shea, mounting an eighty-foot aerial extension in the teeth of swirling cinders, brought down Madame Valois and her maid from a balcony sill moments before the roof rafters fell inward with an ear-splitting crash.

Losses to furniture and crystal chandeliers are estimated at $140,000, substantially covered by Lloyd's underwriters. The management had improvised quarters for all guests at the nearby Wellington Club by daybreak.

RADIO SYMPHONY HEARD ACROSS NATION

Beethoven’s Fifth Broadcast Over Seven-Station Relay With Surprising Fidelity.
— SPECIAL TO THE GAZETTE —

TRANSMITTING WITH a combined etheric strength of twenty-five kilowatts, the Metropolitan Orchestra’s rendition of Beethoven's C-minor Symphony was relayed last night through seven interconnected wireless transmitters, spanning from Boston to the Great Lakes.

The newly developed carbon condenser microphones picked up the delicate pizzicato of forty violins with an absence of customary static crackle that astounded radio enthusiasts gathered at receiver loudspeakers throughout the commonwealth.

SPORTING

BLUE SOX TAKE DECIDER IN 11TH

Before twenty-four thousand screaming spectators at Parkway Oval, the Blue Sox annexed the league flag in the eleventh frame yesterday afternoon when veteran pinch-hitter "Red" Gallagher smashed a whistling liner past third base to score Vance from second. Vance went the full route on the mound, fanning seven and walking only one batter.

COUNCIL IN TUMULT OVER MOTOR LIMITS

Aldermen Clash Over Proposed Twenty-Mile Restriction on Thoroughfares.
— BY OUR CIVIC REPORTER —

SCENES OF HEATED recrimination disrupted the weekly sitting of the Board of Aldermen yesterday when Alderman Higgins introduced an ordinance to restrict all motorcar vehicular traffic within the central precinct to a maximum velocity of twenty miles per hour.

"The boulevard has been turned into a Brooklands speed track by thoughtless youths in sporting runabouts," shouted Mr. Higgins, brandishing police casualty statistics. Alderman Thorndike, speaking on behalf of the Automobile Guild, denounced the measure as "a reactionary retreat to the horse-and-buggy era." Action was postponed until Friday's docket after three members withdrew from the chamber.

SOCIETY

MISS ELEANOR VANE FETED

Over one hundred members of the younger dancing set were entertained last evening at Highbury Manor, the residence of Mrs. Percival Vane, in honor of her daughter, Miss Eleanor Vane. The ballroom was adorned with bronze chrysanthemums and smilax, and music was rendered by a six-piece mandolin club. Miss Vane wore a charming gown of lavender georgette embroidered with seed pearls.

HARBOUR BOARD LOGS RECORD TONNAGE

Eighty-Two Ocean Steamers Clear Customs in Single Fortnight of Harvest Trade.
— MARITIME BUREAU —

FIGURES RELEASED yesterday by Harbour Master Captain Elias Sterling establish a new high-water mark for commerce through the port canal. During the fortnight ending Wednesday, eighty-two ocean-going freighters, representing three hundred and fourteen thousand register tons, passed inward with general cargoes.

Heavy exports of autumn wheat, crated automobiles, and agricultural implements for Liverpool and Le Havre have occupied all available deep-water berths along the municipal quays, requiring three extra harbour pilot cutters on twenty-four-hour duty.
