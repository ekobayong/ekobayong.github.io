Airship Meridian Spans Ocean in Sixty-One Hours

Vessel Lands at Dawn Before 40,000 Cheering Citizens — Commander Reports Calm Skies and Steady Engines Through Night
Special Cable to the Meridian Gazette from Arthur L. Pendelton

THE giant silver leviathan of the skies, dirigible airship Meridian, settled with majestic deliberation onto her mooring tower at the municipal airfield precisely seven minutes past six o’clock this morning, completing the non-stop crossing of the North Atlantic in sixty-one hours and twelve minutes. The triumphant arrival brings to a victorious climax the most daring venture yet attempted in commercial aeronautics.

Under the steady command of Captain Cedric Vance, late of the Royal Naval Air Service, the vessel departed Lakehurst under cover of darkness on Tuesday. Propelled by five Maybach motors of seven hundred horsepower each, she traversed over three thousand nautical miles of unbroken water without a single mechanical mishap.

“We met neither squall nor cross-gale capable of shaking our resolve,” Captain Vance announced through a megaphone to the throngs waiting below. “At midnight over the Grand Banks we caught the northern aurora illuminating our path as bright as noon. Our instruments never faltered, and the envelope maintained complete buoyancy throughout.”

Thousands of early risers had gathered along the river docks and rooftops since four o’clock, alerted by the deep drone of the five propellers carrying miles across the bay. Flags broke out from the tower roofs as the airship dipped her nose in formal salute to the municipality, her vast aluminum-doped hull catching the first crimson rays of sunrise.

Late Wires

Geneva Arms Debate Closes in Impasse

Associated Press Dispatches

GENEVA, Oct. 13. — The preliminary disarmament commission adjourned late this evening following sharp exchanges concerning naval tonnage allocations. Lord Robert Cecil vigorously defended the British proposal for cruiser limitation by class.

M. Paul-Boncour, speaking for France, countered that security pacts must precede material destruction of matériel. Delegates will reconvene Tuesday under gloomy prognostications.

Coal Parley Collapses

LONDON, Oct. 13. — Negotiations between the Miners’ Federation and the colliery proprietors ended abruptly at Downing Street. Prime Minister Baldwin expressed grave regret as winter reserves hit new nadir.

Grand Hotel Swept by Midnight Blaze; Guests Flee in Cold

Plucky Night Porter Saves Score of Lodgers Before Upper Floors Cave In
Staff Reporter

FIRE of unknown origin early this morning gutted the historic four-storey Grand Hotel at Fourth and Commerce Streets, causing damages estimated upwards of two hundred thousand dollars. All fifty-two guests escaped alive through dense smoke, many clad only in nightclothes.

The alarm sounded at 1:18 A.M. from box 42. Night Porter Thomas Brophy discovered flames roaring up the laundry chute and immediately ran along corridors pounding on doors.

Chief Engineer Miller of Engine Co. No. 3 directed seven pumpers against the inferno. Falling masonry crippled a tender, but firemen contained the conflagration before adjoining timber yards took light.

Radio Broadcast Reaches Far Pacific Isles

Symphony Heard Clearly Over Short-Wave by Steamship 4,000 Miles Out

Wireless telegraphy marked another milestone last evening when station WMG’s transmission of Beethoven’s Fifth Symphony, conducted by Maestro Valenti, was received in Fiji with crystal clarity.

Chief Operator H. J. Finch aboard the liner Aorangi logged: “Violins heard with remarkable fidelity; static negligible.”

Sporting Notes

Corbett Stops Dixon in Eighth

Young Corbett won the state welterweight crown last night at the Olympic Arena, flooring “Banty” Dixon twice before referee Jim Blake intervened in round eight.

In baseball, the local Giants closed spring bookings against Newark, guaranteeing twelve exhibition dates for next April.

Council Clashes on Twenty-Mile Motor Speed Limit

Alderman Grubb Calls Fast Roadsters “Scourge of the Public Thoroughfare”

Tempers flared at City Hall yesterday as the Board of Aldermen debated the proposed motor car ordinance. Alderman Horace Grubb introduced an amendment restricting all motor vehicles to fifteen miles per hour in retail quarters and twenty on avenues.

“The reckless speeders have made walking a mortal hazard,” declared Grubb. Alderman Hayes opposed, arguing modern brakes render twenty-five miles wholly prudent.

Harbour Board Sets Record Grain Cargo Marks

Seventeen Steamers Clear Channel with Bumper Western Wheat Crop

Harbour Master Captain O’Leary reported yesterday that the port’s grain elevators cleared 1,420,000 bushels of wheat during the week ending Wednesday, surpassing the prior record established during the war.

British tramp Penrith Castle took aboard the largest individual cargo, clearing for Liverpool with 9,400 tons in her holds.
