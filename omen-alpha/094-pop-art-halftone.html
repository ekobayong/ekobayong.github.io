How the bomb works

Every palette channel is re-screened as a grid of dots on a rotated lattice — the classic CMYK angles offset from your base angle — with dot radius proportional to the square root of the sampled ink density, so midtones bloom into soft seas of colour instead of hard edges. The screens are composited with multiply over the paper colour, the way four printing plates stack on a press.

A Sobel edge detector walks the source luminance and finds the drawing's contours; those black comic outlines are then dilated by stamping the edge layer several times with sub-pixel offsets, so you can fatten them from hairline to marker-pen with the outline slider.

The background is pure Ben-Day: a repeating dot on a 14-pixel grid, the cheapest, loudest, most honest fill in the comic book toolbox.
