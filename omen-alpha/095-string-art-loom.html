A greedy appetite

The weaver never plans ahead. Standing on a nail, it looks at every reachable nail, samples the residual ink along the straight line between them, and simply pulls the thread to whichever chord covers the most remaining darkness. Then the coverage is subtracted and it looks again. It is short-sighted, tireless and — as ten thousand short-sighted steps so often are — surprisingly good.

Why straight lines read as shade

A single taut chord is a hairline. But where a hundred hairlines cross at slightly different angles, the eye stops resolving them and starts measuring density instead: more crossings, darker tone. Curves emerge that no single thread drew. The picture lives entirely in the reader's eye, which is exactly where a picture should live.

Building it by hand

Mark your nails on a 60 cm board, string from the first to the greediest neighbour, keep tension gentle and constant, and loop back around the nail each time. Two thousand chords is an evening; seven thousand is a weekend and a very quiet mind. The algorithm will happily print the visiting order for you.
