Method

The Gray–Scott reaction diffuses morphogen A everywhere while B consumes A to reproduce itself and is suppressed by the kill rate. Turing saw in 1952 that such a simple pair, coupled on a sheet, breaks symmetry into spots, stripes and worms without any blueprint at all — the pattern is the argument.

Handling

Cultures are delicate at the edges of the parameter map. Feed high and kill low and B floods the plate; kill too hard and the skin dies back to clean glass. Nudge the pad a few thousandths at a time and give each culture eight metabolisms to settle before judging it.

Provenance

The classic names — coral, mitosis, worms — descend from Pearson's informal taxonomy of the f–k plane, catalogued by the shape each region grows. Like all good folk classification it was drawn by people who spent long nights watching slides, not by theory.
