Late Edition

Airship Meridian Spans Ocean in Sixty-One Hours

Greatest of the lighter-than-air craft lands at Harbor Field carrying mail, motion-picture film and nineteen passengers — a new eastward record.

By Our Aviation Correspondent

Gliding out of a lavender dawn, the great airship Meridian swung low over Harbor Field at six minutes past seven yesterday morning, sixty-one hours and nine minutes out of the Old World, and settled to her mooring mast while a crowd of ten thousand held its collective breath.

The passage betters the eastward record by nearly four hours, and was accomplished, her commanders report, against head winds of thirty miles an hour on the second night out. The ship crossed the great ocean at an average height of 4,200 feet.

Among those aboard were two Underwriters, a portion of a symphony orchestra summoned for the season's opening, eleven sacks of foreign mail and the first reels of an Atlantic motion picture, developed at once in the Gazette's own darkroom.

“She came home like a gull,” said the landing officer, who watched the hull's shadow slide across the crowd. Planning for a still faster winter schedule is already under way.

Late Wires

Liner Olympic Sightsed Off the Light

The inward-bound liner was reported by the Light keeper at half past four, ninety minutes ahead of her schedule, with all well below.

Miners' Wage Talks Resume Today

Both sides returned to the table at nine o'clock, and a settlement before the week is out is looked for in official quarters.

Late City News

Grand Hotel Fire Roused 300 Guests

An outbreak of fire in the linen room of the Grand Hotel shortly after midnight brought three engines to the corner and three hundred guests into the street in dressing gowns.

The flames were out within forty minutes. Damage is set at £2,000, and the hotel opened its doors for breakfast as usual.

Radio to Give Symphony Tonight

The season's first broadcast symphony goes out from the station at eight, with a Sporting note to follow at half past.

Council & Debates

Council Split on Speed Limit

The Council sat long past midnight on the question of a twenty-mile limit through the shopping streets, and adjourned with the matter uncarried.

The motoring interests were heard at length. A special sitting is called for Tuesday next, when the surveyor's figures will be laid before the members.

Society

Society Notes of the Week

Mrs. Hartwell-Vane opened her winter Thursdays with a small musicale, the Misses Crest attended in silver, and the Ambassador gave a dinner of eighteen covers for the visiting Underwriters.

Engagement is announced of Lieutenant Pryce to Miss Dorset of the Lawn.

Harbour & Amusements

Harbour Board Sets Record Month

The Harbour Board reports the busiest September in its history, with 412 clearances and a grain movement exceeding all expectation.

Two new berths are to be dredged before the spring.

Amusements. — The Winter Garden offers “The Blue Kestrel,” twice nightly. The Regent has the Meridian film. Pantomime rehearsals begin Monday.
