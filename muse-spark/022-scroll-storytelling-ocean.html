From sunlit water to the Challenger Deep — 10,935 metres straight down.

0 – 200 m · Epipelagic

Sunlight Zone

Ninety percent of ocean life crowds into this lit skin. Sardine schools flash like thrown coins; green turtles and manta rays glide on wings evolved for flight.

Photosynthesis rules here — every breath of plankton exhales the oxygen you are breathing now.

200 – 1,000 m · Mesopelagic

Twilight Zone

Blue fades to indigo. Eyes grow huge; bodies grow lamps. The lanternfish rises each night in the greatest migration on Earth, and the moon jelly pulses like a slow heart.

Below ~600 m no sunlight remains — only biology makes light now.

1,000 – 4,000 m · Bathypelagic

Midnight Zone

Permanent night. The sperm whale hunts squid at a kilometre down on a single breath; the anglerfish dangles a living lantern before jaws lined with glass teeth.

Pressure here would crush a car — yet life drifts on, slow and patient.

4,000 – 6,000 m · Abyssopelagic

The Abyss

The seafloor spreads in cold mud. The Atolla jelly, attacked, spins a pinwheel of crimson light — a burglar alarm that summons something bigger than its attacker.

Snow falls upward here: marine snow, the endless drift of the dead feeding the living.

6,000 – 10,935 m · Hadal

The Trench

Hadal — named for Hades. In the Mariana Trench a pale snailfish, all cartilage and hope, swims deeper than any fish should survive, bones unbroken at a thousand atmospheres.

We know the Moon's craters better than this floor.
