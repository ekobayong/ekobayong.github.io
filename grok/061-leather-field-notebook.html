16 September — morning, low tide

The marsh is a sheet of pewter. Sea kale along the wrack line, leaves like pale cabbage, salted. I skimmed stones until the last one hopped seven times and vanished without a splash I could hear.

Bring the bigger sketchbook tomorrow. The light is doing that pewter-to-gold trick again.

Things to remember

The sound of the wreck's rib when the wind is east. How seals arrange themselves like commas. Tide tables with sea lavender pressed between the hours. The detectorist's nod. Redshank, curlew, oystercatcher, egret. Seven hops of a flat grey stone. Bring tea. Come back in October.

— Vol. III closes here. The marsh does not.

15 September — saltmarsh, late

Redshank picking along the gutters, a curlew farther out with that long curved question of a bill. The marsh smells of iron and sweet rot. I stayed until the light went violet and the first lamp came on in the village.

Count: 4 curlew, 11 redshank, 1 hare (maybe).

16 September — dawn

Low-tide morning. The harbour empty as a bowl. I walked the spit again and found sea kale in flower, yellow as a weak sun. Skimmed stones with cold fingers. One of them ran like a hare across the water.

14 September — harbour

Mist on the harbour like breath on glass. Two egrets in the shallows, still as pins, and a pair of oystercatchers piping along the quay. I waited for the foghorn and it never came.

Wind: ENE, light. Tide falling.

14 September — the spit

Walked out the shingle spit. A wreck there, ribs like a whale's, iron blooming orange. Beyond it, seals hauled out and arranged like commas on the wet sand. I counted nine, then eleven when two heads rose from the channel.
