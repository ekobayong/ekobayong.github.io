Airship Meridian Spans Ocean in Sixty-One Hours

Giant dirigible, riding a following gale, drops mail and orchids at the harbour mast before a crowd of forty thousand.

— By C. H. LANSING, Staff Correspondent —

The airship Meridian, silver as a trout and longer than any liner at the quay, completed her first ocean crossing at 4:17 this morning, sixty-one hours after leaving the French field at Orly. Captain H. V. Drummond brought her down through a veil of harbour mist and moored to the new mast as if the Atlantic were a pond. Passengers—twenty-eight of them, among them the soprano Isolde Vane and two members of the Harbour Board—stepped onto the gangway with the dazed courtesy of people who have slept above weather.

A sack of transatlantic mail was flung to the postmen before the engines had cooled. Miss Vane, still in travelling furs, presented the Mayor with a spray of Paris orchids that had, she said, “taken the shorter way.” The Gazette’s correspondent, who made the crossing in the after gondola, reports that the sea was a sheet of hammered pewter for a night and a day, and that the ship’s cook served consommé at an altitude of four thousand feet without spilling a spoon.

LATE WIRES

Cabinet sits till dawn on the coal question

Whitehall. Ministers remained in session after midnight, the Prime Minister declining to speak of a settlement. Pit deputies at Durham telegraph that the men will not go down without a written guarantee of the seven-hour day.

Wireless from the polar schooner Aurora

A faint signal, copied at Poldhu, reports the Aurora fast in new ice but “all well, stove bright, dogs fed.” The latitude given is doubted by the Admiralty, who nonetheless posted it in the hall.

Grand Hotel fire checked at the fourth storey

Flames took the linen rooms shortly after ten. Guests in dressing-gowns stood upon the lawn while the brigade played two streams upon the cornice. No lives lost; a Pomeranian was later discovered asleep in a dumb-waiter.

SPORTING

Radio symphony broadcast from the Civic Hall

Professor Elkin’s orchestra was heard as far as the fishing fleet, who say the slow movement came in “like weather.” In the third the horns wandered, which the wireless men ascribe to a tram.

Meridian Athletic defeated Harbour Rovers 2–1 before a midweek crowd that sang the old rowing air at the whistle.

SOCIETY

Council splits on the new speed-limit

Alderman Crowe would have motor-cars held to twelve miles within the ring road. The younger men spoke of twenty and of “the century we actually inhabit.” The vote is put to Tuesday.

Mrs. Alaric Venn received at the riverside villa. Lanterns were Chinese; the ices, it was remarked, were not.

Harbour Board records a record tide

The gauge at Pier 4 marked 18 feet 3 inches at 3:11 a.m., the highest since the gale of ’19. Lighters were warped inshore. The Board sits this afternoon.

AMUSEMENTS

Palace: Isolde Vane in “Night Mail.” Lyceum: the talking picture demonstration, two reels. Pierrot tent on the West Sands, weather permitting.
